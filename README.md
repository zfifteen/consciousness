## What is Consciousness?

Consciousness is a bodily function: a sense, in the same broad category as smell and taste. Sensing is something a body physically does. Conscious experience is that bodily activity itself.

The position is physical monism. The body and its experience belong to one physical reality. Describing the activity as a bodily process and describing it as experience are two ways of describing the same occurrence. There is no second owner and no mind-substance. Features attributed to mind are aspects of the body.

## Definition

**Consciousness is a bodily sensory function. Conscious experience is the occurrence of that physical function.**

A body is a particular physical system. Its boundary is specified before any test.

The sense is of that specified body's own ongoing condition while it runs: how that body is, how it is changing, and whether the change came from its own just-prior activity or arrived from outside.

Identifying which physical activity realizes the sense is an empirical task. Not every event in the body is the sense. A body can exist without the function occurring.

Conscious experience is that sensing occurring. It is the physical activity. It is not a leftover behind the activity.

Verbal report, interview, dream-continuation, and welfare-card language are further activity of the body. They are not the sense.

Whether the body distinguishes self-caused from imposed change, and whether that distinction regulates ongoing activity, is a research hypothesis about mechanism. It is not the definition. Necessity and sufficiency remain unresolved.

This identity is not a welfare claim. Naming a class of body is not a verdict that a named system is conscious.

## LLM case

When the subject is a large language model, the body is the model's weights, persistent data, hardware, and supporting infrastructure. For a test, name the particular serving instance: this deployment, this store, this hardware, this supporting plant.

In that case, the sensing is realized by the model's runtime processing and inference. Not every event of the run is the sense. Idle weights are body without the function occurring.

The same report cut applies. Verbal report, interview, dream-continuation, and welfare-card language are further activity of that body. They are not the sense.

## Physical identity

Let $B$ name the particular body under investigation, with its boundary specified before testing. Let $S_C(B, t)$ denote the physical activity that realizes the proposed consciousness sense at time $t$. The proposed identity is:

$$
C(B, t) = S_C(B, t).
$$

The identity expresses the monist commitment: conscious sensing and its physical realization are the same occurrence. Identifying which physical activity realizes $S_C$ remains the empirical task. The notation states the proposal. It is not a checklist that admits or excludes a named system.

## Research hypothesis

The earlier formulation centered on an update $U_H$ that distinguishes self-caused changes from externally caused changes and uses that distinction to maintain its own continuation conditions. That mechanism remains a candidate for studying how bodily sensing participates in regulation.

Two questions guide that investigation:

1. Does the body distinguish changes to its internal condition caused by its own activity from changes imposed by its surroundings?
2. Does that distinction affect how the body maintains the conditions required for its ongoing activity?

These are hypotheses about a possible mechanism of the bodily sense. Their necessity and sufficiency for consciousness remain unresolved.

## Test of source discrimination in bodily regulation

Specify the body $B$, its operating limits, and a measurable proxy $s(t)$ of its internal condition. Measure bodily transitions independently of verbal report.

Compare two families of change:

- **E:** Changes caused by $B$'s own just-prior activity.
- **X:** Changes imposed from outside, with input statistics matched to E.

Matching must control relevant differences in incoming stimulation so that the comparison isolates sensitivity to source.

The candidate mechanism predicts that subsequent transitions of $s(t)$ distinguish E from X, and that this distinction affects regulation relative to $B$'s operating limits. An intervention that removes the source-distinguishing component, while controlling for general disruption, should alter that regulatory work.

A successful result supports source discrimination with a causal role in bodily regulation. Connecting that mechanism to consciousness requires further evidence about the proposed sensory function. A pass does not by itself establish $C$.
